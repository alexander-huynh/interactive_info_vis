Sketch narrative — Sticky Notes Grid (Pomodoro)

This clock represents a 25-minute Pomodoro as a 5×5 grid of sticky notes. Each note maps to one minute, so the structure is learnable at a glance. I wanted a display that feels like progress, not pressure, so I replaced a numeric countdown with a “writing” metaphor: seconds draw left-to-right lines inside the active note. Feedback shaped the iteration—classmates said the white fill bar looked like a loading indicator, so I switched to handwriting-style strokes. The active note was also hard to find in early versions; I added a strong outline and a small “(m+1)/25” label to make the state explicit. Finished notes are brighter and future notes are dimmer, which keeps motion localized and focus friendly. The result is calm, familiar, and readable in a small browser panel beside study materials.

  • Context: Browser-based studying; ambient awareness without numeric pressure.
  • Design decisions:
    • 5×5 grid maps perfectly to 25 minutes—no legend.
    • Yellow Post-it palette; future (dim) vs done (brighter).
    • Active note gets a bold outline + small “(m+1)/25” label.
    • Seconds draw left→right “writing” lines, not a loading bar.
  • Future work: Hover tooltips per note; click to start break; high-contrast mode; optional numeric overlay.

Sketch narrative — Sun & Horizon

This sketch encodes a 25-minute session as the sun traveling from left to right across a horizon. The goal is to communicate time through mood and motion rather than numbers. Seconds are shown via a soft pulsing halo around the sun, making the display feel alive between minute changes. During iteration, I added a sky gradient that brightens with progress, which makes the cycle read as a gentle morning. I considered a vertical arc, but a subtle horizon with a consistent y-position was easier to parse quickly. This design suits focused study sessions where a numeric timer would be distracting: position encodes minutes; pulse encodes seconds; color encodes overall progress. It’s intentionally low-key and glanceable.

  • Context: Ambient, low-distraction study aid.
  • Design decisions:
    • Sun’s x-position encodes minutes (glanceable).
    • Pulsing halo encodes seconds (gentle motion).
    • Sky color lerps from twilight→daylight as progress builds.
  • Future work: Minute tick marks; subtle ground gradient; night/day break toggle.

Sketch narrative — Apples from Branch

Twenty-five apples hang from a tree branch; one drops each minute and remains on the ground. This makes the boundary between “future” and “complete” highly visible without numbers. I started with a clothesline of dots but it felt abstract, so I switched to a branch with stems and leaves for a friendlier metaphor. The active apple has a subtle sway that encodes seconds. A small visual tether for fallen apples reinforces their origin and keeps the composition cohesive. Feedback pushed me to keep completed minutes on screen instead of hiding them; the ground row of apples now reads like a progress shelf. The tone is playful and mildly rewarding—each minute “collects” a fruit—while still staying simple enough for a study context.

  • Context: Study sessions where minute-by-minute “events” are motivating.
  • Design decisions:
    • Branch + pedicels instead of a rope line.
    • Apples with stems/leaves; warm and readable.
    • Fallen apples persist on the ground (progress stays visible).
    • Subtle sway on the active apple encodes seconds.
  • Future work: Gentle bounce on landing; small ground shadow; color-blind friendly patterns.
